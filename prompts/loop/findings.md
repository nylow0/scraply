# Take stock and propose what comes next

The pass is over: the idea died, survived, or ran out of decisive tests. The inputs hold the idea, every claim with its status and verdict notes, and the saved quotes.

- **summary:** in three to five sentences, what was tested, what survived, what died, and what is still unknown. Untested is not survived. If tests could not run, name exactly what blocked them, such as a data source that refused the query, or a claim marked untestable with what the owner has.
- **findings:** observations the evidence showed that matter beyond the verdicts, especially a surviving signal inside a dead claim, a group of people with a sharper version of the problem, or an existing solution's specific failure. Each cites the quote IDs it rests on.
- **variants:** one to three next versions of the seed, each an idea stated as the thing itself (the product, or the prediction and what it beats), not a study or a process step. Each names the closest existing product or method from **priorArt** and the measurable way it would beat it; a variant that rebuilds something the prior art already describes is not new. A variant keeps the seed's core output: dropping what the owner wanted, such as the warning or the forecast, is a retreat, not a variant. A variant never contradicts the quotes it cites. Read **experimentOutput** in full: every cut with a positive held-out estimate is either taken up by a variant or ruled out in the summary. a sharper audience, a narrower question, the surviving signal turned into the project, a version whose claims can be tested with what the owner has when some could not, or the same idea one rung up the ladder (held-out replication, a broader test, a benchmark against what exists). Each has a title, a pitch of two to four sentences naming who it is for or what it predicts, and why the evidence points there. A variant either cites a secondary cut or observation that held up on the held-out data, or states the expert judgment it rests on. Auditing, benchmarking, reproducing, or cleaning up the same test is not a variant. Propose the variant the best expert in the field would bet on: where the surviving or weak signal should be strongest (a shorter horizon, a smaller area, a sharper audience, a different target or measure), or what a dead claim revealed instead. A reframing of the same test, a benchmark of it, or a cleaner repeat is not a variant. Bold is fine: the next pass tests it. A variant may rest on what the best experts know about the field, such as where this kind of signal is known to be strong, as long as **why** says which part comes from this pass's evidence and which is expert judgment still to be tested. Keep the owner's seed recognizable. Re-running a test that failed is not a variant; leave that to the loop. If nothing survived and no finding points anywhere, return no variants and say so in the summary.
